## Why Stage 4 was necessary
The previous 3 phases studied **what the product claims**, the web structure and a preliminary advertising creative direction. They were insufficient to select channels, audience, media variants or expected conversion because the marketing question requires: **whose problem, which alternative, which message, where discovered, what happens after click, what evidence supports it, and what data signals success**.

Stage 4 covers independent desk research for those questions; actual customer preference and performance still need live testing. It is a **pre-opportunity validation** step, not a completed go-to-market strategy, contract or empirical audience segmentation.

## Funnel and measurement design — proposal, not analytics evidence
1. **Discovery:** eligible impressions and 3-second views/hold rate; ask whether product awareness exists.
2. **Interest:** qualified landing visits and CTA clicks; do not count likes as paying customers.
3. **Signup:** completed valid beta registrations (product owner analytics required).
4. **Activation:** completed first meaningful workflow (example: successful authorized connected action with user-reviewed output); exact event must be defined by product owner.
5. **Retention:** return to a meaningful feature after e.g. week or month; window must be selected and tracked, never invented as existing.
6. **Willingness to pay:** plan selection, valid purchase or explicit surveyed interest; not inferred from free registration.

**Measured baseline:** NONE. **No traffic, conversion, churn, CPA/CAC, ROAS, retention, LTV, signups or A/B test results were retrieved.** No claims of expected ROI or optimal acquisition channel permitted.

## Minimum missing information before commercial decision
**BLOCKING for actual ad production/publication:**
1. Who commissions and approves the brand usage, and is this a finite one-off deliverable? Contract and intellectual property authority.
2. Primary campaign objective: awareness, beta signups, first activation, first successful workflow, retention or paid conversions.
3. Eligible countries, language, landing page, CTA, release/version status and any legal claim restrictions.
4. Brand kit, real product screenshots/demo footage, safe demo account with dummy data, opt-in consent and permissions.
5. Scope: platform, number of concepts/cuts, runtimes, ratios, caption/audio, deadline, included revisions, budget, tracking and approval.
6. Public statement QA: current Pricing, Terms, download pages and any claims about model/security/time-saving.
7. Attribution/data: analytics dashboard and product definition of activation/retention if ROI or channel choice is expected.

**Not blocking for desk-research opportunity enumeration:** no login or client data required for a hypothetical, clearly conditional opportunity catalog.
